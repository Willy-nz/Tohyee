## p 13 (Eligible expenditure)

"A tax credit is only available on expenditure of $50,000 or more, unless you
use an approved research provider. There is a cap on eligible expenditure of
$120 million in an income year, unless you obtain approval to exceed the
cap."

## p 14 (flowchart and points to note)

Flowchart box: "Foreign R&D expenditure – The amount is eligible to the
extent that the amount is 10% or less of the total claim – see LY 7(5)".
"Contracted R&D expenditure – The amount is eligible to the extent that LY 6
allows (ineligible amounts are stripped out)".
Points to note: "Eligible R&D expenditure is subject to the $50,000 minimum
total spend threshold (section LY 4(1)(a)) and the $120,000,000 maximum total
spend cap (section LY 4(3)(a))." "A person who does not meet the $50,000
minimum total spend threshold may still claim a R&D tax credit for eligible
R&D expenditure that is for an approved research provider …"
Apportionment: "There are no specific rules for how the apportionment must be
applied. It is up to you to apportion the expenditure in a way that is fair
and reasonable, by reference to the advantages you have gained from the
expenditure."

## p 15 (apportionment bases)

"Your apportionment method could be based on any of the following: a
percentage of time; floor area used for the R&D; days/units of usage; volume
used; unit sales; dollar value; activity-based costing principles. Your
apportionment does not need to be made with absolute precision. You must
however be able to point to an appropriate basis upon which your
apportionment has been made, or you will not be able to claim any of the
expenditure."

## p 17 (approved research providers; record keeping)

"If you are carrying out your own R&D activities, you cannot claim the tax
credit unless you have more than $50,000 of eligible expenditure in a tax
year. However, if you have less than $50,000 of eligible R&D expenditure, you
may be able to claim the R&D tax credit if your R&D is performed by an
approved research provider on your behalf."
"Records for your R&D do not need to be kept on paper but as for all other
tax records you are required to retain records, in an accessible state, for 7
years."

## p 62 (Eligible expenditure — LY 5 and schedule 21B part A)

"There is a 10% allowance for expenditure conducted overseas …"
"Depreciation (Schedule 21B Part A Clause 1): Depreciation on assets used in
performing R&D is eligible for the tax credit. The calculation is based on
the time the asset is used for R&D as a proportion of total use.
Availability for use does not factor into the calculation."
Example: a computer used 20% on eligible R&D, 60% on other activities and
available the other 20%: credit on 25% of the depreciation (20 ÷ 80).

## p 63 (unexpired amounts; employees)

Unexpired amounts: "Expenditure you have incurred on a good that has not been
used at the end of the income year (and has not been destroyed) or on a
service that has not been performed, is not eligible for the R&D tax credit.
The amount is eligible once the relevant good or service has been used or
performed."
Overheads listed under schedule 21B part A clause 2: rates, utilities,
insurance, lease payments, security costs, cleaning, repairs and maintenance,
corporate services such as human resources. "An appropriate apportionment
basis must be used and documented."
"Amounts paid to employees (Schedule 21B Part A Clause 3) … This includes:
salary and wages including allowances; bonuses; employee share schemes when
an entitlement arises; employee recruitment and relocation costs; overtime;
holiday pay; long-service pay; superannuation contributions."

## p 64 (apportioning employee time; the Zach example)

"If an employee works on eligible R&D for part of their time, only the
portion of the employee's pay that relates to their time spent on R&D is
eligible. You must demonstrate that you have used an appropriate
apportionment method …"
"Example: Apportionment of employee's time. Zach works on eligible R&D
full-time for 23 weeks of the year. He also attends a 2-week project
management course which is applicable to all his work. He spends 4 weeks on
annual leave, and the remaining 23 weeks doing other work. Zach's employer
can claim 50% of Zach's salary as eligible expenditure … Zach's time spent on
R&D is therefore 50% (23 ÷ 46). … This means that 1 week of the project
management course, and 2 weeks of annual leave are considered eligible. … 23
+ 1 + 2 = 26 weeks. 26 ÷ 52 = 50%."
"If you or anyone else has contributed unpaid time to your R&D, you cannot
attribute a value to those hours … Remuneration must have actually been paid
to be eligible expenditure."
(Shareholder-employee salaries paid at year end are "not normally treated as
payments to employees", and may be treated as services.)

## p 68-69 (contracted R&D)

Legislative reference given as "Income Tax Act 2007 Section LY 6, Schedule
21B Part B Clause 7". Formula: "contract amount − ineligible expenditure".
"Where you engage a contractor associated to you, you can only claim for the
lesser of what you paid the contractor and the contractor's costs. All the
profit margin is removed."

## p 69-71 (R&D conducted overseas; foreign R&D expenditure)

"Expenditure on an R&D activity conducted outside New Zealand is generally
ineligible. However, where you incur expenditure on an R&D activity conducted
overseas, and that activity is integral to a core R&D activity conducted in
New Zealand, your eligible expenditure is the lesser of your actual
expenditure incurred on the activity overseas, and 10% of your total eligible
expenditure." The overseas activity must have general approval as a
supporting activity.
p 70: "Foreign R&D expenditure can only be up to 10% of your total eligible
expenditure." It is where the good or service is consumed that decides
whether it is foreign (imports consumed in New Zealand are New Zealand
expenditure). "Payments for non-residents to perform services forms part of
the 10% cap."
p 71: "Capped amount - 10% of total eligible expenditure. This is calculated
as: (0.1 × total New Zealand R&D expenditure ÷ 0.9)". Example SA Co: NZ
$500,000; foreign spent $250,000; cap 0.1 × 500,000 ÷ 0.9 = $55,556;
eligible foreign = $55,556 (the IR1240 example rounds to the dollar).
"Your total New Zealand R&D expenditure is not confined to the specific
project …"

## p 72 (minimum threshold)

"Legislative reference: Income Tax Act 2007 Section LY 4, Schedule 21B Part B
Clause 24. Your eligible expenditure must exceed $50,000 in the income year to
qualify for the R&D tax credit. There is an exception where you are using an
approved research provider …"
"Where you have more than $50,000 of eligible expenditure in a year, the full
amount will be eligible for the tax credit."
Partnerships, joint ventures and look-through companies may meet the
threshold on the entity's total.
"If your total eligible expenditure is under $50,000, you will only be able
to claim your eligible expenditure in relation to an approved research
provider …" (Hannah example: $10,000 own + $20,000 eligible ARP = $30,000;
only the $20,000 can be claimed.)
Maximum: $120 million including associates' claims, unless approved.

Note for Tohyee: p 17 ("more than $50,000") and p 72 ("must exceed",
"more than") differ from p 13 ("$50,000 or more") and from LY 4(1)(a) of the
Act ("$50,000 or more"). The Act governs.

## p 100-102 (record keeping)

p 100 "Contemporaneous record keeping: For records to be sufficient to support
a claim for the R&D tax credit they should be kept on a contemporaneous or
timely basis. Records which are created after the R&D is finished or at year
end are not likely to meet this requirement. There is no absolute standard
for how frequently a record must be kept for it to be contemporaneous. The
test is whether the frequency is sufficient to provide confidence that the
record is based on reliable information." Time recording, or time
"regularly estimated and reported … for example on a weekly or fortnightly
basis"; for full-time R&D staff, "monthly documentation which identified the
staff involved with the work and the dates …". "If the expenditure on
eligible activities is apportioned, the basis for apportionment must be
reasonable and be supported by an audit trail."
p 101: every business must "keep records for 7 years after the end of the tax
year they relate to"; list of common source documents and R&D records,
including "a worksheet that shows how you calculated the claim expenditure
for each line item - this must reconcile against the amount you have
claimed".
p 102: for foreign R&D expenditure, keep "the currency exchange rate
conversion calculations where applicable". "Where you have apportioned
expenditure, records should clearly show how you have done this. Be prepared
to explain the basis, the reasons why that basis will provide reasonably
accurate results, and the calculation method."

## p 103-104 (claiming)

Legislative reference: Tax Administration Act 1994 ss 33E, 36BE, 36C; Income
Tax Act 2007 ss LY 1 to LY 9. To claim you must: obtain general approval or
criteria and methodologies approval; "file a R&D supplementary return within
30 days after the due date of your income tax return"; file the income tax
return on time ("you can file your return up to a year late, but doing so may
result in penalties and interest"). "If your claim for the R&D tax credit is
to be considered your income tax return must be filed within 1 year after
your due date for filing." Example: return due 31 March 2026 (agent
extension): supplementary return by 30 April 2026, income tax return no later
than 31 March 2027.
p 104: "If you file either return outside of the required timeframes, your
claim will be declined." Both returns must be filed electronically.
Expenditure is reported per R&D project as: materials, consumables and
overheads; R&D tax depreciation; employee related costs; contract
expenditure (approved research provider expenditure separately, once).
