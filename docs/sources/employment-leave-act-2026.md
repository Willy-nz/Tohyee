## s 2 Commencement

(1) This Act comes into force on 6 August 2028.
(2) However,— (a) section 153 and clause 27 of Schedule 1 come into force on
7 August 2026; and (b) section 179 (in so far as it relates to sections 181,
183, and 185 to 187) and sections 181, 183, and 185 to 187 come into force on
1 July 2027.

## s 150 Repeal of Holidays Act 2003

The Holidays Act 2003 (2003 No 129) is repealed.

## s 6 to s 8 Standard, additional and casual hours (extract)

6(1) … standard hours means— (a) hours that, under the employee's employment
agreement, the employer— (i) may require the employee to work; and (ii) must
pay the employee for, regardless of whether the employer in fact requires the
employee to work those hours; and (b) any breaks … for which the employee is
entitled to be paid by the employer; and (c) hours that would have been
standard hours under paragraph (a) or (b) if the employee had not been— (i) on
paid leave under this Act; or (ii) on paid leave under any other legislation
…; or (iii) on leave or on an absence from work [not provided for by
legislation] if … the employer pays the employee …; or (iv) on unpaid leave or
on an unpaid absence from work under any other legislation ….
(2) … an employee's standard hours must be specified in 1 or more of the
following: (a) the employee's employment agreement: (b) a work roster (if
any): (c) the employee's notional roster (if any).
(5) However, standard hours excludes any hours under an availability
provision in the employee's employment agreement.

7(1) … additional hours, in relation to an employee who works standard hours,
means— (a) any hours in addition to the employee's standard hours that the
employer is not required to make available to the employee and that the
employee has the right to refuse and— (i) that the employee works; and (ii)
for which the employer must pay the employee an additional payment; or (b)
any hours that the employee works under an availability provision.
(2) [excludes extra hours of a salaried employee whose agreement says the
salary covers them.]

8 … casual hours means the hours that an employee works if, under the
employee's employment agreement,— (a) the employer is not required to offer
any work to the employee; and (b) the employee is not required to accept any
work offered by the employer.

## s 24 Employee's entitlement to accrue annual leave

(1) An employee accrues not less than 0.0769 of an hour of annual leave for
each standard hour or part of a standard hour.
Example: An employee works 37.5 standard hours each week. The minimum annual
leave that the employee accrues each week is 2.88375 hours, calculated as
follows: 37.5 × 0.0769 = 2.88375
(2), (3), (5) [parental leave standard hours; ASEs accrue on standard hours in
the pay period.]
(4) An employee's entitlement to accrued annual leave remains in force until—
(a) the employee has taken the annual leave; or (b) the employee has been paid
for the annual leave in accordance with section 122 or 123.

## s 25 When employee does not accrue annual leave (extract)

(1) … an employee does not accrue annual leave— (a) for an hour … that the
employee takes as leave or as an absence from work described in section
6(1)(c)(iii) if the employee and their employer agree that the employee does
not accrue annual leave for that leave or absence from work; or (b) for an
hour, or part of an hour, that the employee takes as unpaid leave (other than
any unpaid leave referred to in section 6(1)(c)(iv)); or (c) during a period
for which the employee is receiving accident compensation.
(2) However, if an employee is receiving accident compensation, the employee
accrues annual leave on any standard hours that— (a) the employee works while
receiving that compensation; or (b) the employee takes as paid leave under
this Act ….

## s 73 Employee's entitlement to accrue sick leave

(1) An employee accrues not less than 0.0385 of an hour of sick leave for each
standard hour or part of a standard hour.
[(2)-(4) as s 24(2), (3), (5). s 74 mirrors s 25 for sick leave.]

## s 75 Maximum level of accrued sick leave

(1) An employee may accrue sick leave to a maximum of 160 hours.
(2) If an employee accrues 160 hours of sick leave, the employee is not
entitled to accrue any further sick leave until their accrued sick leave is
less than 160 hours.
(3) This section does not prevent an employee and their employer from
agreeing that the employee may be entitled to accrue additional sick leave.

## s 102, s 103, s 108 Family violence leave

102(1) An employee is entitled to take family violence leave in accordance
with this subpart— (a) if the employee is a person affected by family
violence; and (b) to assist the employee to deal with the effects on them of
being a person affected by family violence.
(2) [regardless of how long ago; even if before the person became an
employee.]

103(1) An employee is entitled, in accordance with this subpart, to family
violence leave on and from their start date.
(2) An employee is entitled to family violence leave for— (a) the 12-month
period starting on their start date; and (b) each subsequent 12-month period
starting on their start date anniversary.

108(1) An employee may take up to 10 days' family violence leave in each
12-month period specified in section 103(2).
(2) An employee cannot carry forward any family violence leave not taken in
any of those 12-month periods.
(3) For the purposes of this subpart, if an employee's work period begins on
one calendar day and continues into the next day, the work period must be
treated as one day.
(s 109: family violence leave may be taken in whole or part days.)

## s 118 Interpretation of subpart 7 (extract): leave hourly rate

leave hourly rate,— (a) for the purposes of calculating a leave payment under
section 120, means,— (i) for an employee who is paid a salary (other than an
ASE), the amount of the employee's salary, including any compensation payable
under an availability provision, that is attributable to 1 standard hour's
work: (ii) for an employee who is paid wages,— (A) for leave taken under
section 34, the lowest hourly rate payable under the employee's employment
agreement; or (B) in all other cases, the lowest hourly rate payable under the
employee's employment agreement for the shift during which the leave is
taken: (iii) for an ASE, the portion of the ASE's salary paid in the pay
period in which the leave is taken divided by the number of standard hours in
that pay period: (b) for … cashed-up annual leave or alternative leave under
section 122, or under section 123 if employment ends, … (i) salary: [salary per
standard hour] (ii) wages: the lowest hourly rate payable under the
employee's employment agreement; but (c) … does not include the following:
(i) LCPs: (ii) allowances: (iii) productivity-based or incentive payments
(including commission)

leave payment rate … means,— (a) if the employee is paid partially or wholly
by piece work, the greater of— (i) the total of the leave hourly rate (if
any) and the average hourly rate for piece work; and (ii) the applicable
minimum hourly rate of pay; or (b) if the employee is paid partially or
wholly by commission, the greater of— (i) the leave hourly rate (if any); and
(ii) the applicable minimum hourly rate of pay; or (c) in all other cases,
the employee's leave hourly rate

(2) average hourly rate for piece work: f ÷ h = p, using piece work wages and
hours worked in the pay periods starting in the 93 days before the relevant
pay period.

## s 119, s 120 Leave payment (extract)

119(2) The employer must— (a) calculate the leave payment … in accordance with
section 120; and (b) pay the leave payment to the employee,— (i) for annual
leave, in the pay period in which the leave is taken, unless the employee and
the employer agree that the employee is to be paid before the leave is taken;
or (ii) for sick, alternative, bereavement or family violence leave, in the
pay period in which the leave is taken; or (iii) for a public holiday [not
worked on an otherwise working day], in the pay period in which the holiday
occurs; and (c) continue to pay the employee any fixed allowances; and (d)
identify the leave payment as a distinct component of the employee's pay in
their leave record in accordance with section 127.

120(2) The employee's leave payment must be not less than the leave payment
rate for each relevant hour that— (a) the employee takes as a period of leave
in a pay period; or (b) the employee does not work on a public holiday that
would be an otherwise working day for the employee.

## s 126 Employer must pay LCP to LCP employee (extract)

(1) An employer must pay an LCP to an LCP employee in each pay period— (a) at
a rate of not less than 12.5% of the employee's ordinary hourly rate for each
relevant hour in that pay period; and (b) in addition to any other payment
the employer is required to pay to the employee for the pay period.
(2) The employer must identify the LCP as a distinct component of the LCP
employee's pay in their leave record in accordance with section 127.
(3) … ordinary hourly rate … means,— (a) [salary]: the amount of the
employee's salary, including any compensation payable under an availability
provision, that is attributable to 1 standard hour's work, excluding
allowances and productivity-based or incentive payments: (b) [wholly wages]:
the lowest hourly rate payable under the employee's employment agreement,
excluding [the same]: (c)-(f) [piece work and commission: compared with the
applicable minimum hourly rate]
relevant hour means,— (a) [standard-hours LCP employee] each additional hour
that the LCP employee— (i) works; or (ii) does not work because they take
sick leave, alternative leave, bereavement leave, or family violence leave:
(b) [casual-hours LCP employee] each hour that the LCP employee— (i) works; or
(ii) does not work because they take alternative leave, bereavement leave, or
family violence leave: (c) [public holiday not worked on an otherwise working
day: the additional or casual hours they would have worked, under s 15]
Example (from the Act): ordinary hourly rate $30; LCP 12.5% = $3.75 per
additional hour; 3 additional hours = $11.25, paid on top of the hourly rate.

## Schedule 1 — transitional provisions (extract)

cl 5 Interpretation: commencement date means the date on which this clause
comes into force [6 Aug 2028] … previous annual holidays means the total of
… (a) the annual holidays to which the employee was entitled after the end
of the most recently completed 12 months of continuous employment under
section 16 of that Act (type A previous annual holidays); and (b) any other
annual holidays to which the employee would have been entitled if they had
ceased employment on the date immediately before the commencement date (type
B previous annual holidays). previous sick leave means … (a) the sick leave to
which the employee was entitled at the end of the applicable period of
employment under section 63 of that Act (type A previous sick leave); and (b)
the sick leave … to which the employee would have become entitled … after the
commencement date; but … reduced to be proportionate to the length of time
[since the start date or the last sick leave entitlement date] (type B
previous sick leave).

cl 6 Existing employers must comply with Act by employee's first pay period.
(1) This clause applies to an employer who employed an employee immediately
before the commencement date. (2) The employer is not required to comply with
provisions of this Act that relate to the employee's entitlements and
payments until the start of the employee's first pay period that starts on or
after the commencement date.

cl 8 Employer must comply with requirements from commencement date. (1) This
clause applies during the period— (a) starting on the commencement date; and
(b) ending on the day before the first anniversary of the commencement date.
(2) The employer must— (a) comply with the employment agreement; and (b)
comply with this Act. (3) However, a term of the employment agreement
prevails if it is more favourable to the employee than an equivalent term
under this Act. (4) This clause does not apply to an employment agreement on
and from the date on which it is amended or replaced if the amended or new
agreement complies with clause 9.

cl 9 Employment agreement must comply with this Act 1 year after
commencement date. [From the first anniversary the agreement must express
annual and sick leave as a proportion of an hour for each standard hour
worked, alternative leave per hour worked, leave payments and public holiday
pay under subpart 7, and LCP under subpart 8.] (3) However, if an employment
agreement does not comply with subclause (2), the minimum entitlements
provided under this Act prevail.

cl 11 On the commencement date, an employer of an employee who works standard
hours and who has previous annual holidays must convert those previous annual
holidays to annual leave hours under clause 12.

cl 12 Converting previous annual holidays to annual leave hours.
(2) type A: w × owh = a, where w is the number of whole or part weeks of the
employee's relevant previous annual holidays, owh is the employee's ordinary
weekly hours, a is annual leave hours.
(3) type B: owh × [(ah × d) ÷ y] = b, where ah is the employee's annual
holidays entitlement under section 16 of the Holidays Act; d is the number of
days since the start date (if employed less than 12 months) or the last date
the employee became entitled to annual holidays under s 16(1), not including,
unless agreed otherwise, days of unpaid leave except unpaid sick, bereavement
or family violence leave or other unpaid leave of no more than 1 week; y is
365 or, if the current year is a leap year, 366 days.
(4)-(6) ordinary weekly hours: the standard weekly hours in the agreement; or
(tsh ÷ td) × 7 over the work cycle; or thw ÷ x, total hours worked or on paid
or unpaid leave in the pay periods starting in the 93 days before the first
pay period that starts after the commencement date, divided by the whole or
part weeks used.

cl 17 On the commencement date, an employer of an employee who works standard
hours and who has previous sick leave must convert that previous sick leave
to sick leave hours under clause 18.

cl 18 Converting previous sick leave to sick leave hours.
(2) type A: d × odh = s (days of previous sick leave × ordinary daily hours).
(3) type B: odh × [(s × d) ÷ y] = b, where s is the employee's annual sick
leave entitlement under section 65(2) of the Holidays Act and d is the number
of days since the start date (if employed less than 6 months) or since the
employee last became entitled to sick leave.
(4) odh = th ÷ d: total hours worked (or on paid or unpaid leave) ÷ total
days worked (or on leave) in the pay periods starting in the 93 days before
the first pay period that starts after the commencement date (or since the
start date if fewer than 93 days).

cl 26 Entitlement to family violence leave. An employee who is not yet
